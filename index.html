01

A changing universe

Stars drift. Stars flicker. Over years, both are measurable.

Illustration

Position · proper motion

Nearby stars travel across the sky by a few arcseconds a year. Too slow to see in a night, it adds up across a decade. Barnard's Star moves the width of the full Moon in about 180 years.

Illustration

Brightness · variability

Some stars pulsate, others hide behind passing dust. Comparing the two SPHEREx sky surveys, wavelength by wavelength, shows whether an object brightened or faded between visits.

02

Observing across time

Two photographs, sixteen years apart. Flip between them and the wanderer gives itself away.

That is how Pluto was found in 1930. WANDERERS does it with infrared images matched by wavelength, so like is compared with like. Pick an epoch on the timeline.

From motion to evidence · How it works

Every claim is a measurement you can see, checked by a rule you can read.

  1. i

    Flipbook across 16 years

    WISE (2010) and SPHEREx (2025, 2026) images of the same patch, in the 3.4 and 4.6 µm channels.

  2. ii

    Gaia ghosts

    Circles mark where every star Gaia DR3 knows should be in each frame. Anything that moves without a ghost is unexplained.

  3. iii

    Measured path

    The target's position is measured in every frame and compared with its catalogue proper motion and parallax.

  4. iv

    Infrared fingerprint

    Aperture photometry in hundreds of SPHEREx images builds a 0.75–5 µm spectrum. Water, methane and CO/CO₂ bands are marked.

  5. v

    Brightness change

    The two SPHEREx sky surveys are compared wavelength by wavelength.

  6. vi

    Evidence card

    Stated rules turn the measurements into a classification and a confidence. No black-box score.

  7. vii

    Plain-language explanation

    Written by Claude from the measurements only, labelled as AI, with rules against over-claiming.

      05

      About

      Candidates, never discoveries.

      WANDERERS was built by Team Hello Universe (Seattle) for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge Planet X and SPHEREx. It points people at interesting objects and shows the evidence. An unmatched object is called a candidate: confirming anything new needs follow-up by astronomers.

      Methods follow the IRSA SPHEREx tutorials: per-pixel wavelengths from the WCS-WAVE table, the conservative bad-pixel flag set, 3-pixel aperture photometry with local background.

      Honest limits

      • Bright stars saturate SPHEREx at many wavelengths; those points are dropped and the card says so.
      • AllWISE is a 2010–11 coadd, treated as epoch 2010.5.
      • Aperture photometry, not PSF fitting: good for spectral shape, not precise absolute flux.
