Sky Compass · for phones

Point your phone. Find the wanderers.

Point your phone's camera at the sky. WANDERERS works out exactly where you are pointing from your GPS position, the time, Earth's rotation and your phone's compass and tilt, then guides you to an object we have investigated with NASA data.

  • Works indoors too: walls don't matter, only direction.
  • Objects below the horizon are shown as well. Point at the floor to find them.
  • Your location and motion data stay on your phone.

Calibrate your phone

  1. True north, automatically. Phone compasses point to magnetic north. We correct this for your exact location with NOAA's World Magnetic Model 2025.
  2. Wake up the compass. Wave the phone slowly in a figure 8 for 5 seconds, away from laptops, magnets and metal desks.
  3. Lock onto something real (best accuracy). Pick a bright object that is up right now, put it in the centre circle on the camera view, and tap the button. This fixes both direction and tilt.

  4. Cloudy or indoors? Two cloud-proof checks.
    Tilt: press the back of the phone flat against a wall or door frame (camera facing the wall), hold still, then tap.
    Direction: point the camera at a landmark you can see. We work out its exact compass direction from your GPS position.

Where the numbers come from

Method: target positions (J2000) are precessed to today (IAU 1976), turned into compass direction and height using UTC time and Earth's rotation (IAU 1982 sidereal time) at your GPS position, with atmospheric refraction (Saemundsson 1986). Sun and Moon use the Astronomical Almanac formulae, with the Moon's parallax. Checked against astropy: stars and Sun within 0.01°, Moon within 0.12°. The phone's compass is the only significant error, which is what calibration removes.
